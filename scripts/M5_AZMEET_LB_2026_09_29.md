## Findings first

- **The library's rule blocked the task.** `IrohLoadBalancer::select_backbone` let everyone forward in rooms of
  up to 8 (`backbone_size`), so a 3-person room could never have a leaf. A mesh cap below 8 needed a knob in the
  library; re-deriving the backbone rule in the app would have duplicated it. Added
  `IrohLoadBalancer::set_mesh_cap(room_size)` (api.json addition below).
- **Peers need one id everywhere.** Connection handles are local, so every report, plan and relayed packet uses a
  peer key: FNV-1a of the endpoint id (`routes::peer_key`), the same on every side. The load balancer ranks by
  score, then by key, so the same reports give the same backbone in any order (tested).
- **Latest-wins frames must not mix streams.** iroh keeps only the newest frame per (peer, track), so a
  forwarder's copy of Ada's audio would replace its own audio, and a 90p JPEG would replace the 360p one.
  Every rendition and every relayed origin rides its own frame track (`routes::frame_track`: lane, rung,
  track); this side's own audio keeps track 3.
- **Headless DPI is 96** (scale 1), so the E2E's expected renditions (grid 200 px, stage 320 px, 360p;
  thumbnail 80 px, 90p) hold. The box heights sit well inside their rendition steps, so a measured height that is
  off by a pixel changes nothing.
- **azul already has the per-user folder**: `FilePath::get_config_dir` (used for the settings file).

## What was built

### 1. Library: `IrohLoadBalancer::set_mesh_cap` (`dll/src/desktop/extra/iroh/loadbalancer.rs`)
The room keeps a mesh cap (8 by default, kept by `Clone`). Up to it every peer forwards; above it
`max(ceil(sqrt N), ceil(N / 8))` peers by score, grown until they carry 1.5 x the fan-out, as before.
`backbone_size` keeps the default. 4 new tests (8 in the file): cap above/at the room size, growth above the cap,
the same reports in any order give the same backbone, a clone keeps the cap.

### 2. `examples/azul-meet/src/routes.rs` (new, pure, 21 tests, no azul types)
- `Sync` (ConnectionSync, kind 6): uplink kbps, stability (thousandths), flags (battery, relay-only, opted out,
  sends audio / camera / screen) and the wants `[origin u64][track u8][height u16]` (the tiles it shows).
  Trailing bytes ignored.
- Relay envelope (kind 7): `[7][1][track u32][origin u64][from u64]` + the inner message or frame as sent.
  `from` = the origin for media, the viewer for a keyframe request or an acknowledgement.
- `Plan::new(peers, backbone)`: leaves round robin over the backbone (best first, leaves in key order); no
  forwarder at all falls back to the full mesh. `children(at, origin)`, `parent(viewer, origin)`, `reaches`,
  `next_hops(at, origin, stream, assigned)` (only children with a viewer of that stream at or below them),
  `edges`. A leaf uploads once and receives everything through its parent; a stream takes at most 3 hops.
- Renditions: `encode_set` (the smallest and the largest height asked for), `pick` (the smallest encoded one at
  least as tall as the tile, else the tallest), `assign(viewers, sender_h264)` (H.264 only when both ends do it),
  `rendition_width` (16:9, even).
- `CapacityEstimator`: per 2 s interval, the larger of bytes sent per second over all connections and the best
  path's cwnd / RTT, in steps (250 kbps .. 100 Mbps), a new step reported after 3 intervals in a row; stability =
  share of the last 15 intervals without >= 3 lost packets or an RTT above max(2 x lowest, lowest + 20 ms), in
  tenths; relay-only = no direct path; a pinned value (`AZMEET_UPLINK_KBPS`) wins.
- Panel lines: `plan_line`, `routes_line`, `role_line`, `peer_line`, `kbps_label`, `stream_label`.

### 3. `video_wire.rs`: the rendition on the wire
Packet header version 2, 20 bytes: `... [frame_no u32][height u16][0 u16]`. Keyframe request
`[3][track][height]`, acknowledgement `[4][track][seq][height]` (height optional on read: 0). Caps bit 1 =
encodes H.264. ALPN `azmeet/3`.

### 4. `lib.rs` glue
- **Reports**: every participant sends everyone its `Sync` on connect, on every change (view toggle, camera /
  screen / mic, peers coming and going) and every 2 s (the stats tick); the tick also measures every tile's
  laid-out height (`get_node_id_by_marker` + `get_node_size`) and the window's scale (`dpi / 96`), and feeds the
  paths' `IrohPeerStats` into the estimator.
- **Plan**: `replan` feeds this side's report and each peer's last one to a fresh `IrohLoadBalancer`
  (`set_mesh_cap(AZMEET_MESH_CAP, default 4)`, `set_peer(IrohPeerCapacity)`, `select_backbone(fan-out)` where the
  fan-out is every open mic to everyone at 2304 kbps plus every video delivery at `rendition_kbps`), reads
  `backbone_peer(i)`, builds `routes::Plan`, logs it when it changes.
- **Sending**: audio frames and each rendition go only to the plan's children (everyone in the mesh; the parent for
  a leaf). One `VideoOut` (encoder, keyframe policy, numbers, counters) per (track, rendition), the encoder at
  `IrohLoadBalancer::rendition_kbps`; per-peer send windows per (track, rendition). The camera and screen widgets
  get one `FrameConsumer` per rendition (the capture pipeline cuts each size); the test pattern is generated at
  each rendition's size. Renditions nobody shows are closed (`stop_culled`); the camera button then reads
  "Stop video (not shown to anyone, not being sent)". The "Drop a video packet" button drops the next packet of
  every rendition this side sends.
- **Receiving**: audio and video are keyed by origin, whichever connection they come through. A viewer decodes
  only the rendition assigned to it (a fresh decoder, at a keyframe, when a stream becomes its own); acks and
  keyframe requests go back the way the stream came (plain to the origin, wrapped to a forwarder).
- **Forwarding**: a backbone peer passes on what its children's viewers need: audio and JPEG as frames on the
  origin's lane, H.264 as messages through a `SendWindow` per (child, origin, track, rendition); a child that
  waits for a keyframe makes it ask upstream (at most once a second). Enveloped acks update those windows;
  enveloped keyframe requests are passed on toward the origin through the connection the stream comes from
  (`[azmeet] Ben: passing Cleo's keyframe request for Ada's camera 90p on to Ada`); the origin logs
  `Cleo asked for a keyframe (camera 90p, via Ben)`.
- **Tile roles**: grid view: every camera `Gallery` (300x200 box); speaker view: the stage peer `Stage` (568x320),
  the others `Filmstrip` (142x80); screens `Gallery`. Toolbar **Speaker view** / **Grid view**;
  `AZMEET_LAYOUT=speaker`, `AZMEET_STAGE=<name>`.
- **Network panel** (a "Network" column of the devices panel, item 5): `Network: 3 people, mesh cap 2: backbone
  Ben, Cleo; Ada uploads to Ben`, `Routes: Ada: Ada>Ben, Ben>Cleo | Ben: Ben>Ada, Ben>Cleo | Cleo: Ben>Ada,
  Cleo>Ben`, `You: leaf, uploading once to Ben`, `You report: up 1 Mbps (pinned; measured 100 Mbps) · stability
  100%`, `Forwarded: N packets, M frames, R keyframe requests passed on`, and per peer `Ben · direct 0.4 ms ·
  backbone · up 50 Mbps · to Ben: audio, camera 90p H.264, camera 360p H.264 · from Ben: Ben audio, ...`. Video
  lines name rendition and forwarder: `Video from Ada (camera 90p via Ben): H.264, decoded ...`,
  `Sending camera 360p: ...`; audio lines add `, via Ben`.
- Other env: `AZMEET_NO_FORWARD=1` (opted out), `AZMEET_ON_BATTERY=1` (no battery API in azul).

## What is left

1. **Routing depth**: the plan is computed on every side from direct reports (everyone is connected to everyone
   here); no single planner, gossip, signed `RoutePlan`, backup parent, per-hop budget (85 % of uplink), 4-hop
   trees, or recruiting non-viewing helpers (routes design 4.3). No stickiness beyond the estimator's steps:
   a rank change moves leaves at once.
2. **Uplink fitting** (a weak publisher steps its top rendition down) and the room-size "adaptive tightening";
   audio culling to active speakers; hysteresis for rendition switches (each switch costs a keyframe).
3. **Estimator quality**: cwnd / RTT overstates on loopback (hence the pinned uplinks in the test) and
   understates an idle link; a real bandwidth probe or BBR's estimate from iroh would be better. No battery API in
   azul (`AZMEET_ON_BATTERY` stands in); no opt-out switch in the UI (env only).
4. **QoS**: forwarded H.264 rides the message stream like the sender's own (see M3's follow-up 2: stream
   priorities / reliable tracks in `IrohEndpoint`).
5. **Meeting server field**: no scheme/port hints beyond the message; the TCP probe at start and the `/health`
   check in the UI are two reachability checks for two moments (before the event loop, and in it).
6. Carried over from M2/M3: Opus, echo cancellation, HEVC/AV1, encoders off Apple, bitrate adaptation, signed
   announcements, library mocks for headless capture / playback.
